    Openpilot Operation¶

    Capabilities¶

    Openpilot is driver assistance, not self-driving. It performs best on highways.

    On supported configurations, it provides adaptive cruise control and automated lane centering. Traffic-light and stop-sign behavior depends on the driving mode, planner, Enhanced Stops and vehicle. Curves and low-speed turns are limited by the vehicle’s steering capabilities.

    You must stay attentive and ready to steer or brake. Driver monitoring helps check your attention.

    Basic Operation¶

    1. Pay attention at all times. You are responsible for your vehicle’s operation.
    2. You can steer, accelerate, brake, or disengage openpilot at any time.
    3. Use your vehicle’s engagement controls; steering and speed can engage separately.
    4. Button and pedal behavior depends on the vehicle and AOL settings. Manual steering or accelerator input may override assistance without disengaging it.
    5. To adjust openpilot max speed, use the CC +/- buttons. Depending on the car, your dash may not reflect the max speed, so instead use the openpilot UI’s max speed shown.

    Always On Lateral¶

    With Always On Lateral (AOL), steering assistance can remain active when cruise speed control is disengaged. AOL can be armed (ready to assist), active or paused. Pedal and Cancel/OFF behavior depends on the vehicle and settings. You can still override steering manually. Use the controls for your vehicle to enable or disable AOL.

    To permanently disable, see settings.

    Lane Changes¶

    1. Verify it is safe to change lanes.
    2. Turn on blinker.
    3. Nudge the steering wheel to initiate the lane change.

    Check your blind spot!

    Openpilot will attempt to use your blind spot monitors on supported cars, but you should always check yourself.

    Lane-change assistance depends on the minimum-speed setting and vehicle capabilities.

    Nudge-less lane changes

    Step 3 can be skipped when Automatic Lane Changes is enabled, after the configured delay and when lane and blindspot checks allow. If Require Cruise for Automatic Lane Changes is enabled, engage factory cruise or StarPilot speed control before signaling. Narrower or uncertain lanes still require a nudge.

    Be careful if you use this non-recommended setting.

    UI Basics¶

    Border Colors¶

    Color Meaning
    Dark blue No active StarPilot steering or speed assistance
    Dark teal AOL armed and waiting to assist
    Teal Steering assistance without active speed control
    Pink StarPilot speed control only
    Green Steering and speed assistance together, or steering with factory cruise
    Yellow Manually selected Chill mode
    Orange Experimental or stop-related state
    Red Traffic Mode
    Purple Switchback Mode
    Grey Manual steering or speed override

    Mode and override colors can take precedence over the steering/speed colors. Alerts and optional turn-signal or blindspot edge indicators can also change the displayed border.

    Max Speed¶

    Your cruise SET speed is shown in the driving UI; its position depends on the layout. SET is the driver’s cruise ceiling. Speed-limit control, curve-speed control or the active planner can request a lower speed.

    Speed Limit Controller¶

    The Speed Limit Controller (SLC) can use map data, dashboard speed limits or camera-detected signs, depending on your configuration.

    When StarPilot controls speed, SLC can limit the target to the accepted speed limit plus your configured offset, up to your cruise SET speed. Accepting a limit normally does not change SET.

    The driving display shows the speed-limit sign and optional offset. Status text explains whether SLC is using the speed limit, using SET, awaiting confirmation or being overridden.

    See the Speed Limit Controller guide for source, offset, confirmation, and override settings.

    Submitting New Speed Limits¶

    If you see an incorrect or missing map-based speed limit, submit an edit to OpenStreetMap. The correction reaches StarPilot after it appears in newer map data and your device uses that coverage. See Speed Limit Maps to download updated coverage for your region.

    Vision Speed Limit Detection¶

    StarPilot can read speed limit signs with the road camera as an SLC source. See the Vision speed limits guide for setup and accuracy guidance.

    In Galaxy, open Toggles → Longitudinal (Speed & Following) → Speed Limit Controller. Select Vision under Primary source, Secondary source or Third source, where available.

    Show speed limit signs controls the display. Enable Speed Limit Controller for accepted limits to affect speed on supported configurations.

    Help us improve it¶

    The model gets better with more real-world driving data. If you'd like to help:

    1. Select Vision as a speed-limit source in Toggles → Longitudinal (Speed & Following) → Speed Limit Controller.
    2. If you also use Maps as a source, install map data for your region.
    3. Drive as normal.
    4. In Comma Connect, open the route, choose Files -> Upload All Files, then under More Info enable Public Access and copy the route ID.
    5. Submit the route through the vision speed limit submission form.

    Let your uploads finish

    Make sure all files finish uploading before turning the device off or disconnecting from Wi-Fi/hotspot. You can confirm in Comma Connect that rlog.zst and fcamera.hevc are present for every segment.

    Curve Speed Controller¶

    The Curve Speed Controller (CSC) uses learned cornering preferences to reduce speed for curves when StarPilot controls acceleration and braking on supported configurations.

    With Show Curve status enabled, the display can show CURVE with its speed target, or CURVE LEARNING with learning progress.

    With CSC enabled on a supported configuration, it can learn on qualifying curves while you control speed yourself, with no lead vehicle tracked and no turn signal active. Qualifying accelerator or brake feedback during a CSC-controlled curve can also adjust saved preferences.

    Longitudinal Planner and Enhanced Stops¶

    Use StarPilot Longitudinal Planner selects the planner for the next drive.

    On supported configurations, Enhanced Stops improves stopping behavior at traffic lights and enables stopping at detected stop signs. Release behavior depends on the stop and the vehicle. If Enhanced Stops is holding the vehicle, press the accelerator to continue when it’s safe.

    On builds that expose Always Hold Enhanced Stops in developer settings, an acquired hold waits for Resume or accelerator input while assistance is active and the required inputs are valid. Disengaging assistance or losing required data can end the hold. Be ready to hold the vehicle with the brake. This option applies on the next drive.

    Experimental Mode¶

    Driving Mode offers Automatic, Chill and Experimental.

    Experimental mode, also known as end-to-end longitudinal (e2e long), lets the driving model influence acceleration and braking on supported configurations. The active planner and vehicle limits still apply.

    Behavior varies with the driving model and conditions. It may brake or accelerate unexpectedly; stay attentive and ready to take control.

    Conditional Experimental Mode¶

    Conditional Experimental Mode (CEM) is presented as Automatic in Driving Mode. It switches between Chill and Experimental using your saved conditions. Configure these under Automatic conditions.

    CEM Widgets and Meaning¶

    The on-screen indicator shows the reason for the current mode choice, such as speed, a lead vehicle, curves, a turn signal or a stop condition.

    Conditional Chill Mode¶

    Older saved Conditional Chill Mode (CCM) selections are handled as Automatic. Current settings use Driving Mode and Automatic conditions, rather than a separate CCM editor.

    Navigation¶

    Navigation displays a route and can help the driving model prepare for turns. It does not drive to the destination for you. Stay ready to steer, especially at intersections and exits. See Navigation & Maps for setup.

    On supported configurations, Turn Signal Guidance can help the model choose a turning path at low speed even without a route. Steering assistance must be active. See Lateral Tuning for the setting.
